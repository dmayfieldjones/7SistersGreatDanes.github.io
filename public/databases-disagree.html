When databases disagree

Updated November 13, 2026 · One real example of how GDGalaxy marks a disagreement.

Pedigree databases make mistakes, and some copy each other's mistakes. Batemanor Amanda at Tiparone (harlequin female, born 1994) is listed in all three databases we link to, and they do not agree.

QuintessaBatemanor Apache War DanceBatemanor Fatal Attractrion at Tiparome
River RockBatemanor Apache War DanceBatemanor Fatal Attractrion at Tiparome
DANES WorldBatemanor Apache War PaintBatemanor Fatal Attraction at Tiparone

What differs

  • Spelling. "Attractrion" and "Tiparome" are typos, and so is "Sulietelma" for Chapala Sulitelma, a few generations back. DANES World has them right. A typo can also split one dog into two records.
  • Parentage. Quintessa and River Rock put Apache War Dance between Amanda and Apache War Paint. DANES World does not. Comparing slot by slot shows fifteen differences, but by who is whose parent there is one.
  • Two against one is not a vote. River Rock repeats Quintessa's typos exactly, so the two probably share a source and count as one witness.

What the site does

It never overwrites a record or picks a side. A difference becomes a flag on the dog's page, listing each version and where it was found. Signed-in members back the version they can support and say how they know. When three different members agree, it is marked resolved. Every vote carries a name, the history stays visible, and the old spelling stays searchable. Open flags are under Needs checking in the top bar.

Where this one stands

The sire is still open. Apache War Dance may be a real dog that DANES World left out, or an extra generation in the other two. A Kennel Club registration or the breeder would settle it. Flags are raised by members for now; automatic comparison across databases is planned.
